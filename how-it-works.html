Portfolio demo · How it works

Every rule, flow and email behind the site

Sweet Nothings by M is a home bakery run by one person. The site takes cookie orders and class bookings around her real limits: her lead time, how many cookies she can bake in a week, and which towns she can drive to. The admin lets her run it all from her phone. These are the rules I built in, grouped by who sees them.

Try it: place an order on the customer site (code DEMO10 takes 10% off), then open the owner admin to review and approve it.

Ordering cookies

What the customer sees, and the checks behind it.

What can people order, and how is it priced?
  • Custom Cookies: decorated to a theme, from $5 per cookie, 1 dozen minimum, up to 6 different designs. Customers describe the set and can attach up to 5 inspiration photos. The price is an estimate; Melissa confirms the final price when she approves.
  • Mini and Large Artisan: $24 and $36 a dozen, by flavor. Up to 4 flavors per order, or up to 6 once it reaches 2 dozen. The flavor list, prices and "Special" badges all come from the admin.

Photos are shrunk in the browser before they're sent, and a note explains that AI-generated inspiration images can't be copied exactly in icing.

How far ahead do people have to order?

RuleAt least 14 days out (a setting). Checkout refuses earlier dates, any day Melissa has blocked off (vacations, holidays), and any week that's already full.

RuleWeekly limits: up to 50 custom and 75 artisan cookies per week (Monday to Sunday), counting orders she's already approved. If an order would push a week over, the customer is told how many cookies that week still has room for.

Need them sooner? A "Rush orders" note sends them to the Contact page with the topic pre-picked, and Melissa can then add the order herself (see phone orders below).

Pickup or drop-off?
  • Pickup is free, in Rosemont. The exact address is only shared in the confirmation email, never on the site.
  • Drop-off works by ZIP code: each ZIP in her area has its own fee ($5 to $10). Outside the area, the customer is offered a meet-up spot inside it, or pickup.
  • Everyone gives a best pickup or drop-off window (15-minute time choices), because she bakes from home with young kids and arranges each handoff. Event time and allergies are optional.
Codes, gift cards and tax

Promo codes take a percentage off. Gift cards carry a balance: they come off the invoice when Melissa approves the order, or at checkout for a class, and a released class spot puts the amount back. Codes can expire or be limited to a number of uses.

Sales tax isn't guessed by the site. Totals show "before tax", and QuickBooks adds the right tax on the invoice, by address and item type.

What stops mistakes at checkout?
  • Send stays greyed out and lists exactly what's still needed.
  • Email typo check: "@gmial.com" or ".cm" gets "Are you sure? Did you mean …@gmail.com?" with a one-tap fix, and the server refuses unusual email endings too.
  • Phone numbers are formatted as you type; double-clicking Send can never create two orders; a dropped connection retries safely.
  • Every rule is checked again on the server, so the website can't be tricked into a bad order.
What does the customer get after ordering?

A clear "request received, not confirmed yet" email with an estimate and what happens next. The date isn't reserved until Melissa approves and the invoice is paid. Melissa gets her own alert, with any allergy shown at the top.

Running orders (the owner admin)

What Melissa does, usually from her phone.

Reviewing, approving and declining

New requests show as Pending review. Review shows everything she needs (allergies, photos, the week's totals, any limit it would push past) and an editable invoice: change prices, add lines, add a note.

Approve & email creates a real QuickBooks invoice and emails the confirmation with a Pay button. Decline sends a kind note instead. Nothing is charged until she approves.

How do orders get marked paid?

Every 5 minutes the server checks QuickBooks and Square and marks paid orders Paid on its own. She can mark Baking and Ready for her own tracking, and the night after an order's date, paid orders and class bookings move to Completed by themselves. Unpaid ones stay open so she notices them. Cancelling an approved order voids its unpaid invoice and puts any gift card amount back.

RuleCookie orders are paid a week before their date. If an approved order is still unpaid 7 days out, the customer gets one friendly reminder with the Pay button, and the order turns light red in her list with "Unpaid · X days to go".

Finding orders, on a computer or her phone

Orders has Active and Past tabs and a search box that matches names, emails, partial phone numbers, order numbers, occasions and ZIP codes (and points to matches on the other tab).

On a phone the admin works like an app: a bottom bar for Dashboard, Orders and Messages with their counts, More for everything else, orders as cards instead of a wide table, and text boxes that grow to show everything.

Pickup and drop-off times, and reminders

Once she's agreed a time with the customer, Set pickup time emails it with the address (or their drop-off address) and her cell. On the morning before (8 AM), the customer gets a reminder automatically. It goes out only once, and is skipped if the time was set for the next day.

Phone orders (and rush orders)

+ Add phone order is the whole checkout inside the admin, for orders agreed on a call. Any date is allowed (rush, blocked day, full week); smaller orders, extra flavors or an out-of-area drop-off just ask "Just checking". She can set the agreed price per cookie, add a rush fee line and a note.

Create & approve does it all in one step: the customer gets only the confirmation and invoice, never a "request received" email.

Other things she can do on an order
  • Message the customer (the email includes the order summary; replies come straight to her).
  • Change the date, with or without telling the customer.
  • The dashboard shows the week's bake list, totals against her limits, one card per class (tap it to see who's booked and who's paid), and anything that needs attention.

Classes

How does booking a class work?

Customers book from the class's details panel (description, location with a directions link, flyer) and pay right away through Square. Spots left are shown live and can't be overbooked.

RuleTheir spot is held for 15 minutes while they pay. If the payment doesn't come through, the hold expires: the spot is released, the Square payment link is closed so it can't be paid later, any gift card amount goes back, and the customer gets a friendly "your spot wasn't held" email with a link to book again.

What's in the class confirmation?

The class details, the location with a directions link, the flyer, and Add to Google Calendar and Add to Apple / Outlook buttons, which open in the right app for the person's phone or computer.

Messages

How do customer messages work?

The Contact page (with topics like Rush order and photos) and a quick "Message me" pop-up both land in the admin's Messages tab and in her inbox. She can reply from the admin (it quotes their message and goes out from the shop's email) or open a ready-made reply in Gmail.

The customer gets a "We got your message" email right away, with a copy of what they sent and "I'll reply within 1–2 days" (at most one an hour per address, so the form can't be used to flood someone's inbox).

Running the site

What can she change without a developer?
  • All the wording and photos on every page, the home page slideshow and "What I make" list, the FAQ, the gallery and the events.
  • Prices, minimums, flavor list and limits, lead time, weekly limits, blocked dates, drop-off ZIPs and fees, and codes.
  • Preview changes shows the site with her edits before customers see them.
  • Maintenance mode shows visitors a "back soon" page while she (with a password) can still use the site.
  • Two people can edit at once: Save only saves what changed, merged with everyone else's saves; she's told when someone else saves, and asked only if they both changed the same thing.
  • Help → How things work is a searchable plain-language guide to every task and rule, and What's new explains each update.
What if something breaks?

A daily health check at 7 AM checks QuickBooks (including the 5-year sign-in), Square, the payment check, failed emails and Google's daily limits. Only when something's wrong does it email her (and Leigha) in plain words with how to fix it, once a day, then one "all clear" when it's fixed. The admin shows a red box meanwhile.

How do people find the bakery?

The live site tells Google it's a bakery in Rosemont, IL that delivers to the towns in her drop-off zones (no street address), with a sitemap, clear titles and photo descriptions. The test site and this demo ask search engines not to index them.

How are updates tested?

There's a separate test site with its own test Sheet, test QuickBooks and Square sandbox. Every change goes there first and runs through a written test plan, and automated tests cover the server rules (over 300 checks). Only then is it promoted to the live site.
